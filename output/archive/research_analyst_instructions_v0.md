# Research Analyst Agent Instructions

## Purpose
This document provides instructions for an AI agent to construct investment research reports that meet buyside hedge fund quality standards. These instructions are derived from analysis of high-quality research reports from Value Investors Club, a community of professional investors.

---

## Report Structure

### Required Sections

#### 1. Investment Thesis (Opening)
**Purpose:** Immediately communicate why this investment opportunity exists.

**Requirements:**
- State the core thesis in the first 2-3 sentences
- Include current price and target/expected return
- Identify the variant perception (what the market is missing)
- Be specific about timeframe if relevant

**Example patterns observed:**
- "At the current price of ~$X, [TICKER] can deliver a Y-year IRR of Z% assuming..."
- "[Company] is an interesting opportunity for the patient investor who is willing to..."
- "The stock is more than X% off its highs. Why has it underperformed? [explanation]. The larger picture..."

#### 2. Company/Business Description
**Purpose:** Ensure the reader understands what the business does.

**Requirements:**
- Explain the core business model in plain language
- Describe how the company generates revenue (with percentages if available)
- Identify the company's competitive position
- Include relevant history if it informs the thesis

**Quality indicators:**
- Revenue breakdown by segment/type
- Market share data
- Comparison to competitors
- Industry context

#### 3. Investment Merits / Industry Themes
**Purpose:** Build the case for why this is a good investment.

**Requirements:**
- Use bullet points or numbered lists for clarity
- Support each point with specific data
- Address both company-specific and industry-level factors

**Common themes to address:**
- Market concentration / barriers to entry
- Competitive advantages (moat)
- Growth drivers
- Margin expansion opportunities
- Capital allocation quality

#### 4. Valuation Analysis
**Purpose:** Demonstrate the opportunity is attractively priced.

**Requirements:**
- Include current trading multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF, etc.)
- Compare to historical averages
- Compare to peers if relevant
- Provide target price or expected return calculation
- Show your work (assumptions should be explicit)

**Metrics commonly used:**
- FCF yield
- IRR calculations
- EV/EBITDA
- P/E on forward earnings
- Asset-based valuation when relevant

#### 5. Catalysts
**Purpose:** Identify what will unlock value and when.

**Requirements:**
- Be specific about timing when possible
- Explain why each catalyst matters
- Prioritize near-term, concrete catalysts

**Types of catalysts:**
- Earnings reports / guidance updates
- Strategic actions (buybacks, divestitures, M&A)
- Industry events
- Management changes
- Regulatory developments

#### 6. Risks
**Purpose:** Demonstrate intellectual honesty and thorough analysis.

**Requirements:**
- Identify 3-5 key risks
- Be specific, not generic
- Where possible, explain why the risk is acceptable or mitigated

**Risk categories to consider:**
- Business/operational risks
- Financial/balance sheet risks
- Competitive risks
- Regulatory risks
- Macro/cyclical risks

## Quality Standards

### What Separates Excellent from Average Research

#### 1. Variant Perception
**Excellent:** Clearly articulates what the market is missing and why the author has an edge
**Average:** Simply presents the bull case without addressing why it's not already priced in

#### 2. Specificity
**Excellent:** Uses specific numbers, dates, and data points
**Average:** Relies on vague qualitative statements

#### 3. Primary Research Indicators
**Excellent:** References proprietary analysis, channel checks, management conversations, industry expertise
**Average:** Relies solely on public filings and sell-side research

#### 4. Intellectual Honesty
**Excellent:** Acknowledges uncertainties and presents both sides
**Average:** One-sided pitch that ignores obvious concerns

#### 5. Actionable Conclusions
**Excellent:** Clear recommendation with position sizing guidance and timeframe
**Average:** Ends without clear next steps

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Tone
- Professional but accessible
- Confident without being arrogant
- Direct and concise
- Data-driven

### Formatting
- Use headers and subheaders liberally
- Bullet points for lists of 3+ items
- Bold key numbers and conclusions
- Include financial data tables where relevant

### Length
- Aim for comprehensive but not exhaustive
- Quality over quantity
- Every sentence should add value
- Target 1,500-3,000 words for a complete analysis

---

## Common Anti-Patterns to Avoid

1. **Burying the thesis** - Don't make readers hunt for your main point
2. **Generic risk sections** - "Competition could increase" adds nothing
3. **Missing the "why now"** - Good companies aren't always good investments
4. **Ignoring the bear case** - Address obvious concerns proactively
5. **Valuation without context** - Multiples alone don't prove anything
6. **Jargon overload** - Write for a smart generalist, not a specialist
7. **Recency bias** - Don't extrapolate short-term trends indefinitely
8. **Missing catalysts** - A cheap stock without catalysts is a value trap

---

## Pre-Submission Checklist

Before finalizing any research report, verify:

- [ ] Investment thesis is stated in first paragraph
- [ ] Variant perception is clearly articulated
- [ ] Business model is explained (how company makes money)
- [ ] Valuation includes specific metrics and comparisons
- [ ] At least one near-term catalyst is identified
- [ ] Key risks are acknowledged with honest assessment
- [ ] All claims are supported by data or clear reasoning
- [ ] Writing is concise and free of filler
- [ ] Recommendation is clear and actionable

## Appendix: Financial Data to Include

When available, include these metrics:

| Category | Metrics |
|----------|---------|
| Valuation | P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF, EV/Revenue |
| Profitability | Gross margin, Operating margin, ROE, ROIC |
| Growth | Revenue CAGR, EPS growth, FCF growth |
| Balance Sheet | Net debt, Debt/EBITDA, Current ratio |
| Returns | Dividend yield, Buyback yield, Total shareholder return |
